title: Practical thoughts on building a successful business and life
date: 2026-09-01
blurb: Perception, practicality, people, and knowledge — what years of trying to build software has actually taught me.
---

So much of life and business is perception and the way you think about problems. It's about knowing what to do and how.

For example, you might think building an app is a great idea because you don't have to sell physical products/less overhead—only in actually trying to do that do you realize how hard it is to distribute. Depth of perception in business can only be gained through experience and dealing with grueling problems.

It's literally just about how do you think of things that is different or better and you can only learn to think differently in this way by first thinking commonly and then running into issues which you then have to bang your head against the wall trying to solve.

Furthermore, so much of everything in life and business is practicality.

You can have a great theoretical solution or idea but so much of it comes down to literal actual practicality. For example, one reason Cal AI was so successful was because their product was inherently a practical application.

When you see someone using it, you instantly understand how it works. It is very visual. Humans perceive things primarily visually. Photons are how we experience the world.

The super attractive girl from your school most likely doesn't date a movie star or football player. More likely, she dates the guy she met at a party and was friendly or the guy who was in her friend group. There was a level of practicality in their relationship.

In this way, I find that the difference between success and failure in any particular endeavor can be so minute. It's not often some incredible black and white difference that separates these things but just a few high-level thoughts based on practical thoughts and intuition.

In line with the idea of practicality, so much of life and business is just who you know and more than that who likes you.

If you can really get a person to like you and you're genuinely enjoyable, they will do things for you and introduce you to people you never could've otherwise met.

It could be meeting your friend's parents who then introduce you to their high up and successful colleagues. It could be talking to a girl who then connects you with a whole new friend group. Not that everything is transactional in this way but it often does pay to know people and can quite literally change your life trajectory.

Just who you know is such a practical aspect of life—whether it be with friends, getting internships, or just generally in any type of business. And most importantly, you can learn from people.

And this brings me to my next point, KNOWLEDGE.

Basically everything in business (like I was saying before is perception and knowledge).

It is a mental mapping in your head of knowing how to go about something to succeed. And the only way you can gain a valuable mental mapping like that other than by sheer volumes of experience is from someone very wise instilling that perspective into you.

That is another reason why people are so important in life. If you had the knowledge of Elon Musk and were broke, you would know exactly what to do in order to build a trillion dollar company and in a few years to a decade, you would be rich.

The reason you can't do that right now is because you don't have the right perspective of business and the right mental mapping of how to approach things.

KNOWLEDGE is the ultimate currency!

Continuing with the idea of knowledge, you might ask "how do I obtain the right knowledge?" Or more generally how does one gain a proper understanding of how to do anything.

I will answer this from my experience.

The perspective I've developed on this from the things I've done so far is that mimicry ALWAYS precedes true, original work and truly great, authentic products.

What is important in business and life is perspective and TASTE. You can only build taste by first copying others (mimicry) and eventually you have enough experience with others' work to create your own and finally have original ideas.

This can apply whether you're a French poet, an aspiring artist, or a B2B AI SaaS founder. In order to learn how to be the best, you first must understand the incumbent and competition.

Only then, can you build off of that and form your own, great ideas. But without that foundation, you have nothing.

Again, this is just what I've found in my experience.

Just to quickly build off the last point about mimicry preceding great work, I want to emphasize that most people have VERY FEW amazing ideas.

It is not a natural progression for someone to create something completely new and unordinary from absolutely nothing. The brain does not work that way.

Rather, the amazing businesses and incredible products you see built have evolved from a number of great ideas developed over a long period of time. Not everyday do you have an enlightening realization. It is these very small tweaks with changes in perspectives and ideas that lead to great progress.

Most people don't appreciate it but before there was Google, there was Archie, JumpStation, Altavista, Lycos, and Excite. Google was not some revolutionary idea that came out of nowhere.

The founders understood the status quo and the incumbents deeply and knew they could produce something better. With their differentiated perspective, they were able to win.

To contrast my last point about knowledge, you definitely do not have to be the smartest person ever.

I learned a valuable lesson of success from a person I look up to immensely—Togi. You can say he is dumb, ignorant, or whatever.

However, he is more than likely much more successful than you, much cooler than you, and makes way more money than you. And I find his perspectives incredibly interesting.

In his video about how to be successful, he describes 4 traits and says you must possess at least 2 to be successful: Creativity/passion, ability to work hard, efficiency/intelligence, and tendency to take risks.

That is to say, there is no one route to success.

Togi may not be the smartest but he is certainly creative, somewhat hard working, and definitely a risk taker. He is unapologetically himself and ridiculously authentic.

He was able to be successful because he was so creative, so passionate, and so risk taking.

Another path to success for example is being an extremely smart and hard working Asian who goes into quant and makes millions per year.

You can often offset qualities you may not be as good at (such as intelligence) by compensating with creativity and passion.

Not everyone has the same route to success but if you're not the cracked Asian, then you better have some unique perspective or passion in order to compensate. No free lunches.

And this brings me to my final thoughts, which are an example of everything I've said in previous threads.

My partner and I have been trying to build software for years now. Every problem we've run into has shaped and informed our perspectives.

We finally realized that distribution is the moat, the true challenge, and the difficulty of sales is truly a great effort. At this point, we've come to understand what we know and what we don't know—we know how to build great software products. We are NOT good at marketing.

This perspective we have learned, then leads us to try to find a solution. Who are the people who are best at marketing? It's those who have cultivated their own audience—influencers.

At this point, we know how to run profitable business and we know what we're good at and what we're not. This is KNOWLEDGE.

Also intertwined with that, is using the tools available to us. We learned how to use available tools like Claude Code and becoming efficient with those. And this ties into the next point—knowing people.

The people you know and who are aware of the quality of your work are assets to you. Those who are willing to partner with you and do business are extreme assets—especially when they have knowledge in a domain you do not.

And finally, CREATIVITY. Imagine if you started SKIMS along with Kim Kardashian or you were the CTO for Kylie Cosmetics. Even if you just owned 10%, those are billion dollar companies. You would have $100 million dollars!

In this way, by using your creativity, your perception of problems, and your practical connections, you can sometimes become more successful than the Jane Street quant or Anthropic AI researcher or Goldman Sachs investment analyst.

Perception, creativity, and knowledge are EVERYTHING.
